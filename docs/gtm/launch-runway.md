# GTM Runway: Aug 1 -> Sept 1 drop (built 2026-07-31)

Produced by the GTM crew, then adversarially critiqued. THE CRITIC'S FIXES ARE PART OF THE PLAN — read both.

## Week-by-week

### Week 1: Aug 1-7 — Foundation and warm ignition — Flip the site to waitlist mode, light up the warm network, get the content engine breathing
- Confirm waitlist mode is live on berrova.com: countdown running, 'Pour first' capture working end to end (test with your own email), thank-you page asks for one forward
- Personal 1:1 outreach wave 1: 15 warmest DFW + aviation contacts, individually written texts/emails (AI drafts, Kyle personalizes and sends). Ask two things: join the waitlist, forward to one person who loves coffee
- Activate Instagram and TikTok handles: bio, link to waitlist, 3 grid posts establishing the brand (palette, JACRA cert story, 'Most Blue Mountain is faked. Ours is proven.')
- Publish first 2 Reels from the drafted real-vs-fake scripts using cert documents, maps, price comparisons as visuals — no product footage needed yet
- Load the X queue (Airtable+n8n) with 2 weeks of education posts: the fake-JBM problem, JACRA certification, what real JBM costs and why
- First 2 coffee-shop scouting visits (see visit kit) — relationship visits, not sales calls
- Set up the weekly metrics row in Airtable: waitlist count, outreach sent/replied, visits, content posted, hours spent

### Week 2: Aug 8-14 — Education engine at full cadence — Real-vs-fake content series running on rhythm; local circuit expands; first waitlist email
- Hold the content rhythm: 3 Reels (cross-posted to TikTok), 5-7 X posts from queue, 15 min/day founder replies on X
- Warm outreach wave 2: next 15 contacts, plus follow-up nudge to wave-1 non-responders (one nudge only)
- 3-4 coffee-shop visits; book return visits for the week of Aug 18 ('samples land mid-month, can I bring you one?') and log every contact in the B2B pipeline table
- Waitlist email #1: founder note — who Kyle is, why this brand exists, the fake-JBM problem, what happens Sept 1. Plain, honest, no hype
- Publish the 'How to spot fake Blue Mountain' checklist as a page/post with waitlist CTA — the evergreen lead asset
- One founder post on LinkedIn introducing the brand and the certification angle, pointed at the corporate gifting page for B2B eyes
- Pitch 2-3 DFW food newsletters/blogs with the education angle (local founder, most Blue Mountain is fake, here's how to tell) — organic press, no spend

### Week 3: Aug 15-21 — Samples land, content gets real — First physical product in hand changes everything: sample drops, real footage, tasting content
- The moment samples arrive: shoot a full day of raw footage (unboxing, beans, brewing, packaging) — this bank feeds every Reel through the drop
- Deliver samples to every shop that said yes; set a specific follow-up date with each ('I'll come back Tuesday, tell me honestly what you think')
- Reels shift to product + education hybrid: cupping the real thing, what certified JBM actually tastes like, side-by-side with a grocery 'Blue Mountain blend'
- Waitlist email #2: 'Samples just landed. Here's what 200 bags looks like.' Real photos, real countdown, restate that waitlist pours first
- Aviation network push: personal messages to pilot/crew contacts framing the gift box and the story — people who fly to Jamaica know the Blue Mountains
- Warm outreach wave 3: remaining warm contacts plus everyone who replied warmly but hasn't joined
- 2-3 new shop visits, now with samples in hand — the strongest version of the visit

### Week 4: Aug 22-28 — Conversion push — Turn attention into waitlist commitment; explain exactly how the drop works
- Publish the drop mechanics honestly everywhere: 200 bags, opens Sept 1, waitlist gets the link first. That is the whole pitch — real scarcity needs no dressing up
- Sample follow-up visits to every shop: collect honest feedback, ask the wholesale question directly, log outcomes (yes/maybe/no + why) in the pipeline table
- Waitlist email #3: 'How the drop works' — exact open time, pricing, subscription option, what waitlist members should expect in their inbox Sept 1
- Best-of content week: re-cut the 2 best-performing Reels angles with product footage; X thread telling the full founding story with the cert as the spine
- Final personal ask round: text the 20 warmest people directly — 'Drop opens Monday, you're on the list, forward this to one person who'd care'
- Follow up on press pitches; offer any interested writer a sample and an interview
- Prep drop-day assets: launch email, launch posts for X/IG/TikTok, thank-you/sold-out variants written in advance

### Week 5: Aug 29 - Sept 1 — Drop week — 72-hour countdown, then open the doors to the waitlist first
- Daily countdown content: 72h / 48h / 24h posts across all channels, all pointing to the waitlist while it can still get people in the first email
- Sept 1: waitlist email at open time with the direct purchase link, then public posts 2-3 hours later — the 'pour first' promise kept, visibly
- Founder live on socials during opening hours: real-time honest updates only (bags remaining is fine to share because it is true; never inflate urgency)
- Personally message every shop and wholesale conversation: 'We're live, here's the wholesale sheet' — the DTC drop is the proof slide for the B2B pitch
- Log every order source (waitlist email, X, IG/TikTok, in-person, referral) so the post-drop retro says which channel actually sold coffee
- Post-drop within 48h: honest recap content — what sold, what's left or when the next drop is, thank-you to buyers by channel

## Channels
- **X (existing Airtable+n8n queue)** (5-7 posts/week automated via queue; 1 thread/week; 15 min/day founder replies): Daily education posts from the real-vs-fake JBM spine: certification facts, price economics of genuine JBM ($70-130/lb retail), how blends get labeled, plus founder build-in-public notes. One longer thread per week. Every post carries the waitlist CTA. Kyle spends 15 min/day replying in coffee conversations as himself.
- **Instagram Reels + TikTok (same content, cross-posted)** (3 Reels/week per platform, batched in one weekly 2-hour production block): The drafted real-vs-fake scripts are the spine. Weeks 1-2: education-only visuals (certs, maps, price receipts, text-on-screen) since no product is in hand. From mid-Aug: real product footage from the sample shoot day — brewing, cupping, side-by-sides. Bio link goes to the waitlist. Zero extra production for TikTok; it gets the same files.
- **Waitlist email** (1 email/week Aug 8 onward, plus drop-day send): Four sends before the drop: founder story, samples-landed with real photos, how-the-drop-works, and the launch email itself (waitlist gets the buy link before public posts). Written in brand voice: direct, honest, no hype. Confirmation email asks each member to forward to one person.
- **Local DFW in-person (specialty coffee shops, roasters)** (3-5 visits/week (roughly 2-3 hours including drive time)): Founder walks in as a customer first, has a real conversation, books a sample return visit for mid-Aug, leaves a card with the waitlist QR. After samples land, the visit becomes a tasting. Every contact logged in the B2B pipeline table. This channel feeds both wholesale and the most credible word-of-mouth.
- **Warm network (DFW + aviation)** (10-15 personal messages/week; one follow-up nudge max per person): Personal 1:1 texts and emails in three waves, AI-drafted but Kyle-personalized and Kyle-sent. The ask is small and specific: join the waitlist, forward to one coffee person. Aviation contacts get the Jamaica/gift-box framing. This is the highest-conversion channel a zero-audience brand has; treat it as the primary channel, not a side play.
- **LinkedIn (founder profile)** (1-2 posts/week, 20 minutes each): Low-cadence B2B surface for the corporate gifting and wholesale pages: founder posts about building a certified-authenticity coffee brand, aimed at gifting buyers and office managers in the network. Not a growth channel; a credibility channel that makes warm B2B outreach land better.
- **Organic local press / newsletters** (2-3 pitches in week 2, follow-ups in week 4; ~1 hour/week): Pitch DFW food newsletters, blogs, and community groups with the education hook: local founder, most Blue Mountain sold in the US is fake, here's how certification proves the real thing. Offer samples and an interview once product is in hand mid-Aug. Even one placement beats a week of social posts for a zero-audience brand.

## Waitlist growth plays
- Warm 1:1 ask with a forward multiplier: every personal message asks the contact to join AND forward to one coffee person. At warm-network reply rates this is the single highest-yield play available and costs only Kyle's sending time.
- Every piece of content ends at the waitlist: X posts, Reels, TikToks, LinkedIn posts all close with the same true line — '200 bags. September 1. Waitlist pours first.' Real scarcity stated plainly needs no embellishment.
- The 'How to spot fake Blue Mountain' checklist: a genuinely useful education asset published with a waitlist CTA (or delivered via signup). It converts the content spine into a capture mechanism and is evergreen after launch.
- Waitlist confirmation email asks for one forward: 'Know one person who'd care that most Blue Mountain is fake? Send them this.' A built-in, zero-cost referral loop with no fabricated incentive needed.
- QR waitlist card handed out in person: every coffee-shop visit, aviation contact, and casual conversation gets a physical card pointing at the waitlist. In-person signups from people who met the founder convert to buyers at the highest rate.
- 'Pour first' as a real, kept privilege: publicly commit that the waitlist gets the buy link hours before anyone else on Sept 1 — then keep it visibly. The waitlist must be worth joining for a concrete, honest reason.
- Aviation-network angle: personal notes to pilot and crew contacts who know Jamaica and the Blue Mountains firsthand — they are both credible buyers and natural word-of-mouth into a high-income, gift-buying community.
- Founder-as-human participation in DFW coffee communities (local subreddits, coffee Facebook groups, events): show up transparently as the founder, answer questions about JBM authenticity, never astroturf. Slow, but compounding and free.
- Local press/newsletter placements pointed at the waitlist: any article or newsletter mention links to the waitlist page, not the (closed) store — the countdown page is the only destination that can capture the traffic anyway.

## Metrics (honest targets)
- Waitlist signups per week (the north star): honest targets W1 20-35 (warm network), W2 15-25, W3 20-30, W4 25-40, drop week 20-40. Total by Sept 1: 100-180. Anything over 200 is outperformance, not the plan.
- Warm outreach: 10-15 personal messages sent/week, reply rate 40-60% (they know Kyle), waitlist conversion of repliers 50%+. If replies are high but signups low, the ask is broken, not the audience.
- In-person circuit: 3-5 shop visits/week, cumulative 12-16 real conversations by drop, 5-8 booked sample visits, 2-4 live wholesale conversations by Sept 1. Zero wholesale POs expected before the drop — the DTC sellout is the proof that closes them after.
- Reels/TikTok: 3 posts/week held for 4 straight weeks (consistency is the metric). Honest view expectations on zero-audience accounts: 200-1,000 views typical, occasional outlier. Track saves and shares over views; follower counts under 500 by drop are normal and fine.
- X queue: 5-7 posts/week shipped, 1 thread/week, replies-per-post trending up. Expect under 500 followers by Sept 1; measure profile-link clicks to the waitlist instead of follower count.
- Email: open rate 50-70% and click rate 15-25% are honest for a small warm list — if opens fall below 40%, the list is being grown with the wrong people.
- Drop-day honesty check: 10-20% of the waitlist buying in week one is a strong result (15-35 orders, roughly $1,500-3,500 at current AOV). A day-one sellout of all 200 bags is NOT the target and should never be implied in content; selling through over the following weeks with wholesale layering in is the realistic win.
- Founder time: log actual hours weekly against the 10-hour cap, split content / outreach / in-person. If any week exceeds 10, cut the lowest-performing channel — the cap is a constraint, not a suggestion.
- Attribution: every signup and order tagged by source (warm 1:1, X, IG/TikTok, in-person, press, referral) so the week-after-drop retro ranks channels on evidence, not impressions.

## THE CRITIC'S VERDICT (apply these)

### Flaws found
- Time budget is broken by roughly 2x. Adding the stated cadences: 15 min/day X replies (~1.75 hr), 3 Reels/week batched (2 hr, optimistic for someone who has never produced a Reel), 3-5 shop visits/week (2-3 hr with drive time), 10-15 personal messages personalized and sent by Kyle (~2 hr), weekly waitlist email (~1 hr), plus LinkedIn, press pitches, metrics logging, and week-specific one-offs (account setup, checklist page, drop-day asset prep). That is 12-18 hrs/week against a <10 hr ceiling, before anything slips. The plan as written guarantees the founder silently drops tasks, and the plan does not say which ones to drop.
- The 200-bag launch inventory has no arrival date anywhere in the plan. Samples arrive ~mid-August, but nothing confirms the actual sellable 200 bags land, get packaged, and are ship-ready before Sept 1. The countdown is public and the brand's entire positioning is honesty; a supplier slip on the main order kills the launch date and there is no contingency line item, no order-status checkpoint, no slip plan.
- Week 2 promises shops samples ('samples land mid-month, can I bring you one?') before the samples exist. Supplier ETAs of '~mid-August' slip routinely. If they slip even one week, Kyle has made and broken a specific promise to the exact B2B relationships the plan calls its most credible channel. Weeks 3-4 (sample drops, product footage, waitlist email #2, sample follow-up visits) all single-thread through this one uncontrolled date with no fallback sequencing.
- Instagram/TikTok are treated as a load-bearing channel but the accounts start at zero followers on Aug 1. Three Reels/week for four weeks on brand-new accounts will reach almost nobody by Sept 1; the algorithm ramp alone takes longer than the runway. The hours spent there are hours taken from the warm network and shop visits, which the plan itself admits are the highest-conversion channels. This is effort misallocation dressed as a content strategy.
- The 'side-by-side with a grocery Blue Mountain blend' Reel is a defamation/disparagement exposure if a recognizable brand or label appears on camera while Kyle implies it is fake. 'Most Blue Mountain is faked' is a defensible category claim; pointing the claim at an identifiable product is not, and the plan gives no guardrail.
- Week 5 assumes assets that do not exist and time Kyle does not have: a 'wholesale sheet' is referenced for the first time on drop day with no build step anywhere in the four prior weeks, and 'founder live on socials during opening hours' assumes Sept 1 daytime availability the <10 hr/week constraint says he does not have (day job). Drop-day fulfillment (packing, labels, shipping workflow for up to 200 orders) is also entirely unplanned.
- CTA variant 2's rationale fabricates an asset: it claims to use 'the existing Skip the line language' but the site's verified language is 'Pour first' and the countdown/waitlist copy; 'Skip the line' appears nowhere in the brand facts. For a brand whose rule is no fabrication, shipping copy justified by an invented precedent is disqualifying, and 'skip the line' also mildly overstates the mechanic since every waitlist member gets the same early access.
- The press pitch timeline is near-zero probability as scheduled: pitching DFW food newsletters in Week 2 for coverage that matters before a Sept 1 drop gives writers 2-3 weeks, with no sample in hand to send until Week 3 at the earliest. Fine as a free lottery ticket, but the plan schedules follow-up as if it is a real channel.

### Fixes
- Cut the plan to fit 10 hours with an explicit priority stack: (1) warm network 1:1s, ~3 hr/week, untouchable; (2) shop visits capped at 2/week, ~2 hr; (3) waitlist email, 1 hr; (4) X queue runs automated with replies cut to 10 min/day, ~1 hr; (5) Reels cut to 1/week in Weeks 1-2 (education, text-on-screen, cheap to make) rising to 2/week only after product footage exists. Everything else (LinkedIn, press, checklist page) becomes a 'only if hours remain' list. Write the drop rule into the plan: when a week overflows, channels die from the bottom of the stack, never the top.
- Add a Week 1 action: confirm the 200-bag production and delivery date with the supplier in writing, and set a go/no-go checkpoint on Aug 18. If bags are not confirmed ship-ready by ~Aug 27, either move the drop date publicly and early (on-brand honesty, announced as such) or switch Sept 1 to a preorder-with-ship-date model. Never let the countdown outrun the inventory.
- Reword the Week 2 shop script to remove the date promise: 'I'll bring you a sample as soon as my first shipment lands, should be mid-August, I'll text you the day it arrives.' Add a slip branch to the calendar: if samples miss Aug 21, Weeks 3-4 collapse into one conversion week, product Reels are replaced by more education content, and waitlist email #2 becomes a transparent 'here's exactly where the shipment is' founder note, which the honesty positioning actually makes stronger.
- Demote IG/TikTok from channel to archive: post the Reels (they cost one batch block) but set zero expectations and zero extra hours; their real job in this window is making the brand look alive when a warm contact or shop owner checks the profile. Move the reclaimed hours to more 1:1 messages and one extra shop visit, and measure waitlist source in Airtable so the September retro proves or disproves this with data.
- Add one production rule for comparison content: never show a competitor's brand name, label, or identifiable packaging; compare 'certified single-origin JBM' against 'a typical $15 grocery blend' generically, with the JACRA cert and price receipts as the visual evidence. The category claim carries the whole argument without the legal exposure.
- Move drop-day asset creation forward: build the one-page wholesale sheet in Week 2 (it is also useful in every shop visit from Week 3 on), and in Week 4 prep the fulfillment workflow (packaging materials on hand, label/postage process tested with one dummy shipment, realistic ship-by promise stated in the launch email). Replace 'founder live during opening hours' with pre-scheduled launch posts plus one honest evening update with real numbers.
- Kill CTA variant 2 or strip its false premise: if 'Skip the line' is used anywhere, it must be introduced as new copy on its own merits, not justified as existing site language. Default the site to variant 0 sitewide and reuse variant 1's fact-first framing ('200 bags. September 1.') on the product page where buyers want specifics; both stay inside verified facts.
